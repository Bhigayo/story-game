## Part 1. The brief (copy from here)

I'm building a story-driven open-world crime drama, and I want your version of the story. Here's exactly
what the game is and what it can do, so your story fits what can actually be built.

**What it is.** A 3D game that runs in a web browser: low-poly and stylised, a warm golden-hour palette,
GTA-style third person. It's set in a real, accurately mapped part of **Birmingham, UK**: **Digbeth** (old
industrial streets, railway viaducts, the canal, the Custard Factory arts quarter, The Old Crown pub from
1368, the coach station, street art, nightlife) and the **city centre** next to it (the Rotunda, the
Bullring, New Street, Chinatown and Southside, Corporation Street, the Colmore business district). The
streets, buildings and landmarks come from real map data, and the shops carry their real names.

**Who it's for.** Gen Z, UK. Grounded, not cartoonish. The tone is British crime drama (think Top Boy,
Blue Story, Snatch, with Birmingham's own identity), with humour and heart. No glamorising: choices have
costs.

**It's single-player.** The city is yours alone, so the story can change it: a raid, a heist, a building
sold or saved can stay changed for the rest of the game.

**Several protagonists you switch between, like GTA V.** You play more than one character, each with their
own life, home, car, money, contacts and missions. You can switch between them in free roam: the camera
pulls up over Birmingham and drops down to the other one, who's somewhere doing something in character. Some
missions belong to one character, some bring two or three together and let you switch mid-mission (one
drives, one goes inside, one keeps watch). Their stories pull them together and against each other. Right
now there are two placeholders, **Jordan Hayes** and **Maya**: keep, change or replace them. Tell me how
many protagonists the story needs (two or three) and why. Each one costs a fully animated model and their
own storyline, so don't add one without a reason.

**Existing cast** (keep, change or drop them, but give reasons):
- **Leon Campbell**: mechanic and hustler at a garage yard; funny, always another way to earn. Gives you a sofa to sleep on.
- **Aisha Rahman**: runs an independent café and community space; practical, outspoken.
- **Nia Okafor**: ambitious recruiter and business developer, organised.
- **Raymond Ellis**: long-standing local resident; sarcastic, knows the area's history, distrusts outsiders.
- **Daniel Mercer**: executive at **Meridian Developments**, a fictional developer buying up the
  fictional **Foundry Yard** in Digbeth. Redevelopment brings jobs and money, and also rising rents and
  pushes independents out.

**Existing Act 1** (called "The Collision"; it can change): you arrive and sleep on Leon's sofa → you meet
Aisha and Raymond → Leon asks a favour, and your answer matters → "The Night It Went Wrong": something
happens at a lock-up near a courier depot. The first heist planned is on **Meridian's site-office safe**.

**What the game can already do** (missions should be built from these):
- On foot: walk and run anywhere; talk to characters with dialogue choices that change relationships
  (-100 to 100) and set story flags; a phone with a map and GPS, missions, bank and texts.
- Driving: get into parked cars, steal them, or **carjack** traffic (drag the driver out); cars, vans
  and black cabs; damage, smoke and wrecks; horn.
- Police: patrols that witness crimes; a 1-to-5-star wanted level; pursuit cars that box you in;
  **officers chase on foot**; lose them by breaking line of sight; busted means fined.
  Crimes: theft, carjacking, hitting people, red lights and speeding in front of police, ramming.
- A living city: traffic that follows lights and give-way rules, pedestrians who cross on the green
  man and dive out of the way, day and night, rain, an area-name banner (DIGBETH, BULLRING...).
- Coming next: health and hospital respawn, a mission framework (markers, trigger zones, fail and
  retry, checkpoints), the phone as a hub (texts from characters start missions), a small safehouse,
  saving everything. After that: a planned heist (a planning board, crew roles, approach choices,
  an escape), and light combat (fists, no guns at first).

**Constraints.**
- All businesses, organisations and people are invented: no real brands, no real people. Real places are fine.
- No guns early on. Violence is rare and has weight. Combat stays light.
- A small number of meaningful decisions with lasting consequences, not branching on every line.
- Scope: about 3 acts, 6 to 10 missions each, plus side jobs. Free roam is always available.

**What I want back.**
1. A title idea and a one-line pitch.
2. How many protagonists, who they are, and why (background, what they want, what they're hiding).
3. The full cast: who's needed, their roles, and which ones are recurring and need their own model.
4. The central conflict and the antagonist(s).
5. Act-by-act structure, with a mission list for each act. For each mission: name, location (a real
   Digbeth or city-centre place), what you do (using the abilities above), and the story beat.
6. The 4 to 6 big decisions and what each one changes later.
7. Endings (at least 3).
8. How switching fits: when and why you'd switch, which missions use two or three of them, where each one
   lives and what they're doing when you switch to them, and any moment where they're on opposite sides.
9. Side activities that fit the world.

## Part 2. My pitch, to compare against

**Title idea:** *Foundry Yard*.
**One line:** Two strangers who owe the same dangerous man have one autumn to pay him, while a
developer bulldozes the Digbeth that raised them.

**Two protagonists you switch between (a third later).** Keep Jordan and Maya, and make them more than
placeholders: **two people with two reasons to be in the same mess.**
- **Jordan**: back in Birmingham after a few years away. Leon's cousin. Behind on rent for a mum who's being
  "decanted" from a flat Meridian has bought. Good with cars, bad with saying no.
- **Maya**: from the area. Works at Aisha's café and studies at night. Her older brother is inside, and
  owes **Kieran Doyle** (an invented local operator: legit scrapyard and car wash, not legit everything
  else). She's quietly paying his debt off.
- You play both and switch between them, GTA V-style. Jordan's life is Leon's garage, cars and Doyle's
  jobs; Maya's is the café, Raymond and Aisha's campaign, and her brother's debt. **Heists use both**:
  switch between the driver outside and the one inside. A third protagonist could be added in Act 2:
  **Nia**, playable from the moment she turns on Meridian, giving the inside view of the development.

**Act 1: The Collision** (mostly what's already built). Arrival; Leon's sofa; the café; Raymond's warnings.
Leon's "small favour" is moving a van for Doyle. The night it goes wrong: the lock-up by the courier depot
is raided, the van disappears, and now **Jordan owes Doyle for the van**. Maya was there too, for her
brother's debt. Ends with Doyle making you both an offer: work it off.

**Act 2: The Debt.** Jobs for Doyle: stealing cars to order (a list, real streets), getaway driving, a
car-wash "collection", losing the police with a hot car. Meanwhile Meridian's Daniel Mercer is buying
everyone out, and Nia offers you a legit way out (a job on Meridian's "local talent" scheme). Raymond and
Aisha are organising against the redevelopment. Mid-act twist: **Doyle and Mercer are working together.**
Doyle scares the leaseholders out; Mercer buys the empty units cheap. The proof is in the safe in
Meridian's site office.

**Act 3: The Yard.** The heist on Meridian's site office, with a planning board, the crew (Leon driving,
Aisha's knowledge of the site, Nia's access pass, or your own approach), and an escape through Digbeth.
What's in the safe: cash, the ledger of payments to Doyle, and planning documents. Then the big decision:
**what you do with the ledger.**

**The big decisions (examples)**
1. Leon's favour: do it, refuse it, or do it and tip off Aisha.
2. Nia's offer: take the legit job (it gives you access later) or turn it down. (If Nia is a third protagonist,
   this becomes her own turning point.)
3. Who you protect when the lock-up goes wrong: Leon or Maya's brother.
4. Doyle's "special job" (threatening a café owner): do it, fake it, or warn them.
5. The heist approach: quiet (Nia's pass), loud (smash and grab), or inside job (Leon).
6. The ledger: hand it to Raymond and the press, blackmail Mercer, or sell it to Doyle.

**Endings**
- **Out clean:** debt paid, Doyle exposed, Foundry Yard saved, but you leave Birmingham.
- **Take the yard:** you replace Doyle. Rich and feared, and alone.
- **The long game:** you blackmail Mercer and get a stake in the development. The neighbourhood changes anyway.
- **Burned:** it all collapses; one of the two goes down for it.

**Cast that needs its own model (10):** Jordan, Maya, Leon, Aisha, Nia, Raymond, Mercer, Doyle, Maya's
brother (Act 2, prison visit), and Doyle's enforcer. Everyone else is crowd.

**Side activities:** cars to order (a phone list), Leon's garage jobs (deliver and fix), the café's late
deliveries, Raymond's history walks (unlock landmark stories), street races (later), helping
independents with "lost customers".
