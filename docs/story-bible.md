# THE CITY TAKES: story bible (v1, 23 Nov 2026)

Working title: **The City Takes** (alternatives: Brummie, No Fixed Address; check UK IPO, Steam and domains
before choosing). Merged from ChatGPT's "Brummie" outline and Claude's "Foundry Yard" pitch.
The old life-sim bible ("Room to Make It") is retired; its life-sim systems are in `archive/life-sim/`.

**The game:** a single-player, story-driven open-world crime drama in a real, mapped Digbeth and Birmingham
city centre. Three protagonists you switch between, GTA V-style. Grounded British crime drama: humour,
heart, and choices that cost something. No guns early on; violence is rare and has weight.

**One line:** three lives collide in a changing Birmingham, where a redevelopment deal, a missing package
and one disastrous night pull a young driver, a courier and a property insider into a conflict that
threatens everything they've built.

**Theme:** everyone has a price, but not everyone knows what theirs is.
**The question the ending asks:** not whether you can change Birmingham, but whether you can live with the
Birmingham you've made.

## The conflict

Meridian Developments is buying up the properties around **Foundry Yard**, Digbeth. Some of what it promises is
true (jobs, units, public space). It's also using shell companies, dodgy valuations and undisclosed payments,
and pressuring businesses to sell before the public understands. Aisha's café is on the list.

**What actually happened** (one sentence): months ago Meridian made an unofficial payment to speed up an
acquisition; a courier was hired to move a **copy** of the ledger that proves it, the package went missing on
the night it all went wrong, and **the original is in the safe in Meridian's site office.**

Who wants it: Meridian (Rooke) wants it back. Daze wants to sell it. Jordan wants out of his debt. Maya wants to
know what she was carrying. Adrian knows what's in it, but not who has it.

**The real question:** who gets to decide what Birmingham becomes, and who pays for it.

### The heist (mission 15) is the centrepiece
Three roles, switchable throughout. Three approaches, chosen in mission 14:
- **Insider:** Adrian's pass gets them in through the office (but he might be recognised).
- **Distraction:** Jordan draws the security and police off (a heat-raising stunt), and Maya goes in.
- **Quiet:** Maya goes in by a back route over the yard; a timing and route puzzle.
No guns. The tension is timing, being seen, noise and the police response.

## Endings (the city stays changed; free roam continues)
- **A City for Its People:** Meridian exposed, Foundry Yard goes community-led. The café survives; Adrian loses his
  job but rebuilds; Jordan's debt is cleared; Maya starts a legitimate local courier business.
- **A Better Deal:** a negotiated compromise. Some businesses protected, some moved on. Aisha loses part of her
  space; Adrian keeps his career at a cost; Jordan and Maya leave with money and complicated friendships.
- **The City Takes:** Meridian wins. The café closes, Leon struggles, Adrian gets his promotion, Jordan gets paid
  and loses the people who trusted him, Maya leaves Birmingham.
- **No Fixed Address** (hidden): strong relationships with all three, plus the full money trail. They expose the
  whole network and walk away without becoming the city's new power brokers.

In free roam afterwards: the Foundry Yard site is sealed, redeveloped or partly preserved; the café and the
garage are open or shut; characters are friends, enemies, or won't speak to you.

## Side activities (no gambling, no random violence)
- **Jordan:** Leon's garage jobs, Daze's cars to order, driving challenges, debt encounters.
- **Maya:** courier work (some jobs are suspicious: you choose), community events, family visits, photographing murals.
- **Adrian:** corporate assignments, property inspections, networking.
- **Anyone:** Raymond's history walks (landmark stories), canal towpaths, hidden cash, newspaper clippings
  about the redevelopment, time trials, police escape challenges.

## The everyday moments
Between the big missions: Jordan on Leon's sofa, Maya arguing with Aisha about something trivial, Adrian eating
alone after work, Raymond complaining about how Digbeth has changed. These are what make the big moments matter.

## Build order (story side)
1. **Switching** between the three (built v47), with each character's own position, cash and wanted level.
2. Mission framework (markers, trigger zones, fail and retry, checkpoints), phone texts that start missions,
   save everything.
3. **Vertical slice: Act I missions 1 to 4** (coach station to Colmore Row) with one decision and a visible
   consequence. Then test: does switching feel good? Is the city recognisable? Are the three interesting?
4. Act I 5 to 8, Leon's garage as a safehouse, homes for Maya and Adrian.
5. The heist (missions 14 and 15), then Act II, light combat, Act III and the endings.
